Brand photography · Visual positioning strategy

The photographs your company gets recognised by.

A company has a face too. You see it in its leaders, its team, the atmosphere of the workplace and the small details people inside may no longer notice. Those are the pieces I bring together.

The same approach also works for individuals. Executives building a public presence, entrepreneurs launching something new and artists presenting their work all need images that function in real situations.

I do not want to hand you a folder of beautiful photographs that no one knows how to use. I create a practical image library for your website, press, recruitment, presentations and campaigns.

How the work is built

Long-term visual partnership

I learn about the company first. The photographs come next.

Before planning a shot list, I spend time understanding how the company actually works: who carries its authority, where its culture becomes visible and which situations matter to customers, candidates and partners. The photography day should begin with decisions already made, not with guesses. The same discipline applies whether the subject is a corporate leadership team, a founder building a new venture, an artist positioning a body of work, or an individual building a personal brand.

1 · Understand

We begin with the organisation or individual, its goals, culture, audiences and the situations — corporate, entrepreneurial, artistic or personal — in which the visual presence must work.

2 · Position

We agree on how the people and the organisation should come across, then turn that into a clear visual direction.

3 · Create

Executive portraits, brand photography, employer-branding imagery and event documentation are planned to work together.

4 · Roll out

The image system is adapted for LinkedIn, websites, press, presentations, recruitment, campaigns and international communication.

5 · Evolve

When leaders, teams or communication needs change, the visual language can be updated without starting again.

From positioning to a practical image library

I begin with identity, audience, market context, differentiation and the current gap between how the brand wants to be understood and how it is presently seen. Together we define the visual territory: level of formality, emotional distance, authority, accessibility, environment, colour, light and recurring motifs.

The photography then translates this positioning into a usable structured image set. Founder portraits, team situations, workplace details, products, editorial scenes and campaign images serve different channels, but communicate the same strategic position.

Project frameworkThe practical things worth agreeing before we start+

What does the project fee cover?

Before I photograph anything, we put the scope in writing: preparation, the shoot, selection, retouching, the final fee and any real travel or production cost. Work on location in Vienna and Budapest is included; anywhere else is agreed beforehand.

What arrives, and when?

You should not have to guess what happens after the shoot. The written offer sets the preview, selection and final-delivery dates, together with the file formats you actually need.

What happens to private material and image rights?

One person on your side can collect approvals so feedback stays simple. Unreleased photographs stay confidential. Your usage follows the agreed licence, and I do not turn client work into a public reference without permission.

What if the date changes?

Once the offer is accepted and any agreed booking payment is made, the date is yours. If plans move, the accepted offer already tells us how rescheduling, cancellation and documented outside costs are handled.

Different roles need different visual authority.

There is no universal executive image. A founder, a senior banking leader, an established adviser and a specialist entering a leadership role communicate different things. We shape brand photography and visual positioning around the person, generational communication context, level of responsibility, professional field, audience and usage channels — never around a generic business-portrait template.
